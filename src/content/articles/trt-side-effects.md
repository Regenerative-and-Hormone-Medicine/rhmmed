<p>You've read the threads. One guy's hematocrit went so high he has to give blood every few months. Another says nobody warned him his testicles would shrink. Someone else swears TRT wrecked his skin.</p><p>Meanwhile you're crashing by 2 p.m., your drive has gone missing, and the gym stopped paying you back. You're shorter with the people at home than you want to be, and you've started wondering if this is just who you are now.</p><p>So you're stuck between two worries: staying like this, or starting something you've heard can go sideways. Both make sense. Wanting to understand TRT side effects before you commit is the right instinct.</p><p>Here's what most of those stories have in common. Someone treated a testosterone number, and nobody stopped to ask what was driving it.</p><h2>Why So Many Men Get a Prescription Before Anyone Asks Why</h2><p>Maybe you've already been through it. A quick online visit, one blood test, a vial in the mail. Nobody asked how you sleep, what your stress looks like or whether you want kids. Nobody checked in at three months.</p><p>That's how care is often set up, and it tends to treat each piece on its own:</p><ul><li>Fatigue gets one answer.</li><li>Low drive gets another.</li><li>The weight around your middle gets a diet.</li><li>Poor sleep gets put down to stress.</li></ul><p>What if they're connected?</p><p>Low testosterone is often a symptom with a cause behind it. Poor sleep, extra body fat, insulin resistance, chronic stress, thyroid problems and some medications can all pull it down. The Endocrine Society's guideline asks clinicians to confirm low testosterone with repeat morning blood tests and to look for those causes before treating (<a href='https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29562364/' target='_blank'>Bhasin et al., Journal of Clinical Endocrinology &amp; Metabolism, 2018</a>).</p><p>When the cause gets skipped, it doesn't go away. It stays in the background and often shapes which side effects show up once treatment starts.</p><h2>How RHM Approaches TRT Side Effects</h2><p><strong>We're not here to convince you that you need TRT. We're here to figure out why you don't feel like yourself anymore.</strong></p><p>That means our care starts with you as a whole person. Your testosterone result tells us where to start looking. You tell us the rest: how you sleep, how you feel at 3 p.m., what's changed at work and at home, and what you want the next year to look like.</p><p>Then we look under the symptom. Our full panel reads 160+ biomarkers across hormones, thyroid, metabolism, inflammation and nutrient status, so we can see what is pulling your testosterone down and what could make treatment harder for you specifically.</p><p>Sometimes the answer is TRT. Sometimes it's TRT alongside treating sleep or insulin. Sometimes it's fixing the cause first. You can see how the program is set up on our <a href='/men/hormone-therapy'>men's hormone therapy page</a>.</p><p><strong>If this sounds like you,</strong> a VIP call is where care with RHM begins. We'll listen to what's going on, talk through your concerns about treatment and map out an evaluation that looks at you as a whole. <a href='/scheduling'>Book a VIP call</a>.</p><h2>The Things That Lowered Your Testosterone Also Shape Your Side Effects</h2><p><em>Sleep, body fat, insulin and stress don't stop mattering once treatment starts. They help decide how your body handles it.</em></p><p>It's easy to think of side effects as luck. Most of them follow from four things testosterone does in the body, and your own health decides how strongly each one plays out.</p><h3>1. It quiets your brain's signal to your testicles</h3><p>Your brain senses the testosterone coming in and turns down LH and FSH, the hormones that tell your testicles to make testosterone and sperm.</p><p><strong>What makes it matter for you:</strong> whether you want children, and when.</p><h3>2. Some of it becomes estradiol</h3><p>An enzyme called aromatase, especially active in fat tissue, converts part of your testosterone to estradiol.</p><p><strong>What makes it matter for you:</strong> more body fat and insulin resistance usually mean more conversion, which raises the odds of breast tenderness and fluid retention.</p><h3>3. It tells your bone marrow to make more red blood cells</h3><p>That helps your energy, and it's also why hematocrit can climb too far.</p><p><strong>What makes it matter for you:</strong> untreated sleep apnea can push red blood cells up by itself, so the two can stack.</p><h3>4. It acts on tissues with androgen receptors</h3><p>Skin, hair follicles, the prostate and your breathing during sleep all respond to it.</p><p><strong>What makes it matter for you:</strong> your skin history, family history of hair loss, prostate health and how well you breathe at night.</p><p>That's why two men on the same dose can have completely different experiences. When we know your sleep, weight, metabolism and goals before you start, most side effects become things we plan around, and your labs confirm we're on track.</p><h2>Common TRT Side Effects (and What Usually Fixes Them)</h2><p>You may worry that any change you notice means something has gone wrong. Most early changes are mild, and they respond to small adjustments when someone is paying attention.</p><h3>Acne and oily skin</h3><p>Breakouts on your back or shoulders can make you feel like a teenager again, and not in the good way. Testosterone increases oil production in the skin, so this is common in the first months. Most cases are mild and settle with standard skin care or a dose change.</p><p><strong>What else it can be:</strong> blood sugar swings, poor sleep and stress also drive breakouts, so we read your skin alongside your <a href='/men/metabolic-health'>metabolic health</a>.</p><h3>Fluid retention</h3><p>You might notice puffier ankles, a tighter ring or a jump on the scale in the first weeks. It's usually mild. When it lingers, your physician looks at your dose, blood pressure and estradiol together, along with your weight and diet.</p><h3>Breast tenderness or gynecomastia</h3><p>This is the one many men are most embarrassed to ask about. Breast tenderness or growth is seen in roughly 10–25% of men on TRT, and the balance between estradiol and testosterone matters more than how high testosterone itself goes (<a href='https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3897047/' target='_blank'>Osterberg et al., Indian Journal of Urology, 2014</a>).</p><p>Caught early, it usually responds to a dose or schedule change. When estradiol does need medication, that's a physician call based on your symptoms and labs, because pushing estradiol too low brings its own problems with libido, joints and bone. Our guide to <a href='/articles/estrogen-dominance'>estrogen dominance</a> explains how estradiol behaves in men.</p><h3>Injection-site soreness</h3><p>If needles aren't your thing, this one's on your mind. A sore spot or small lump after an injection is common and short-lived. Technique, needle size and rotating sites usually fix it, and we'll walk you through all three.</p><h2>Serious Side Effects of TRT, and How We Catch Them Early</h2><p>“Serious side effects” can make it sound like you're rolling dice. They're the reason follow-up care exists, and most of them can be spotted early when someone is paying attention to both your labs and how you feel.</p><h3>High hematocrit (thicker blood)</h3><p>This is the one you're least likely to feel, which is why it matters who's looking after you. Testosterone drives red blood cell production, and if hematocrit climbs too high, the blood thickens and clot risk rises.</p><p>Guideline-based reviews say treatment should be paused if hematocrit rises above 54% and restarted at a lower dose once it normalizes. The American Urological Association acts at 52% (<a href='https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12052019/' target='_blank'>Basheer et al., International Brazilian Journal of Urology, 2025</a>).</p><p>The usual first fix is a lower dose given more often, which smooths out the peaks that drive red cell production. If sleep apnea is part of your picture, treating it can help too. Therapeutic blood donation is available when your physician thinks it's needed. It isn't the default plan.</p><h3>Worsening sleep apnea</h3><p>If you snore, wake up unrefreshed or fight to stay awake in the afternoon, read this one twice. Testosterone can worsen obstructive sleep apnea, and the Endocrine Society lists untreated severe sleep apnea as a reason not to start TRT.</p><p>In our clinic this comes up constantly. Our medical team estimates that more than three in four men who come to RHM for low testosterone have sleep apnea or significant sleep problems. Poor sleep lowers testosterone by itself, so looking at sleep first can change the whole plan.</p><h3>Blood pressure and cholesterol</h3><p>If heart disease runs in your family, you've probably thought about this already. Testosterone can nudge blood pressure up and shift your lipid profile. Both are checked at follow-ups, alongside the habits and history that affect them.</p><h3>Prostate changes and PSA</h3><p>Many men assume TRT causes prostate cancer. Current evidence doesn't show that in carefully monitored men. Testosterone can enlarge the prostate and raise PSA slightly, so we record a baseline PSA before starting and track it against your own number afterward.</p><p>A rise beyond what's expected leads to a urology referral. The Endocrine Society also advises against starting TRT with active prostate or breast cancer, or a PSA above 4 ng/mL (above 3 in higher-risk men) without further evaluation.</p><h3>Mood changes</h3><p>Your partner may notice this before you do. Most men on TRT describe steadier mood and more patience. Some feel irritable or more emotional, usually when levels swing between injections. Shorter intervals between doses often smooth this out, which is one reason we ask how you've been feeling at every visit.</p><h2>What About Your Heart?</h2><p>For many men, this is the question that keeps them from starting. For years the evidence was mixed and the headlines were frightening.</p><p>The TRAVERSE trial gave a much clearer answer. It enrolled 5,246 men aged 45 to 80 with low testosterone and existing heart disease or high cardiovascular risk, and followed them for about 33 months.</p><p>Heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death occurred in 7.0% of men on testosterone and 7.3% on placebo (<a href='https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37326322/' target='_blank'>Lincoff et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2023</a>).</p><p>Atrial fibrillation, acute kidney injury and pulmonary embolism were more common in the testosterone group. TRAVERSE also used a daily gel with levels kept between 350 and 750 ng/dL, a steadier pattern than some injection schedules produce.</p><p>So the heart picture is reassuring, with three specific things to watch: heart rhythm, kidney function and clot risk. Your history tells us how closely to watch each one.</p><h3>One finding worth knowing: fractures</h3><p>You might assume testosterone protects your bones. In a TRAVERSE sub-study of 5,204 men followed for a median of 3.2 years, clinical fractures occurred in 3.50% of the testosterone group and 2.46% of the placebo group (<a href='https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38231621/' target='_blank'>Snyder et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2024</a>).</p><p>The practical lesson is that testosterone on its own is an incomplete bone plan. Vitamin D, strength training, protein and fall risk still matter, and we look at them as part of the same picture.</p><h2>Fertility, Testicle Size and Stopping TRT</h2><p>If you want kids someday, or you're worried about shrinking, this is probably the section you came for.</p><p>When testosterone comes from outside, your brain lowers LH and FSH. Sperm production drops, and the testicles can get smaller. The Endocrine Society recommends against starting TRT if you're planning a pregnancy in the near term.</p><p>For most men it's reversible. In a pooled analysis of 1,549 men from hormonal contraception studies, sperm counts recovered in 67% within 6 months of stopping, 90% within 12 months and essentially all within 24 months (<a href='https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16650651/' target='_blank'>Liu et al., The Lancet, 2006</a>).</p><p>Recovery was faster after shorter treatment and with shorter-acting testosterone. Those men started with normal testosterone, so your timeline may differ. That's why we talk about your family plans in the very first conversation.</p><p><strong>Can you stop TRT once you start?</strong> Yes. Your own production takes time to restart, and the symptoms that brought you in can return while it does. If stopping is ever the plan, your physician can map out the transition with you.</p><p>Some men who want to keep fertility fully open look at other options first, which we compare in <a href='/articles/peptide-therapy-vs-trt'>Peptide Therapy vs TRT</a>.</p><h2>Side Effects of Testosterone for Women</h2><p>Testosterone is part of some women's hormone plans, and you may be wondering whether it would change your voice or your hair.</p><p>The Global Consensus Position Statement on testosterone for women found that at doses matching a premenopausal woman's natural range, the side effects were mild increases in acne and body or facial hair in some women. It found no hair loss, clitoral enlargement or voice change at those doses (<a href='https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6821450/' target='_blank'>Davis et al., Journal of Clinical Endocrinology &amp; Metabolism, 2019</a>).</p><p>The strongest evidence for benefit is for low sexual desire after menopause. The key phrase is “natural range”: women's doses are a small fraction of men's, and regular check-ins keep levels there. Learn more about <a href='/women/hormone-replacement-therapy'>hormone replacement therapy for women</a>.</p><h2>When TRT Isn't the Right Call, We'll Say So</h2><p>You might expect a TRT clinic to say yes to everyone. If testosterone doesn't fit you, we'll tell you, and we'll explain why.</p><p>We may hold off, or treat something else first, if you have:</p><ul><li>Untreated severe sleep apnea</li><li>A high hematocrit before treatment</li><li>Active prostate or breast cancer, or an unexplained high PSA</li><li>A heart attack or stroke in the last 6 months, or uncontrolled heart failure</li><li>Plans to conceive soon</li><li>Low testosterone driven mainly by sleep, weight or another fixable cause</li></ul><p>For many men, treating sleep or weight first lifts testosterone enough that they need less help, or none.</p><p><strong><em>Dr. Rand Insight:</em></strong></p><div data-rt-embed-type='true'><blockquote class='blockquote'><p><em>“A testosterone number tells me where to start looking. The main serious risk is a high hematocrit, followed by worsening sleep apnea, blood pressure changes and prostate changes. Most can be caught early with regular follow-up.</p><h3>How soon do TRT side effects start?</h3><p>Injection soreness and skin changes can appear in the first weeks. Hematocrit and PSA changes usually develop over the first 3 to 6 months, which is why follow-up labs are timed then.</p><h3>Do TRT side effects go away?</h3><p>Most improve with a dose, schedule or delivery change. Sperm production and testicle size usually recover after stopping, over months.</p><h3>Can TRT cause hair loss?</h3><p>Some testosterone converts to DHT, which may speed up thinning in men already genetically prone to male-pattern hair loss. Men without that tendency rarely see it.</p><h3>Will I have to donate blood on TRT?</h3><p>Not usually. If hematocrit climbs, the first step is typically adjusting the dose or how often you take it, and checking for sleep apnea. Blood donation is a backup when your physician thinks it's needed.</p><h3>Can you stop TRT once you start?</h3><p>Yes. Your natural production takes time to restart, so stopping works best when it's planned with your physician.</p><h2>Ready for TRT Care That Starts With Understanding You?</h2><p>You've spent long enough weighing how tired you feel against what you've heard about side effects.
